Utah short-term rental hosts deal with two layers of tax on every booking: sales tax and transient room tax. Airbnb and VRBO handle most of the collecting, which is great, but it also means the numbers on your payout don't look like the numbers on your booking. If your books just record the payout as "rental income," they're wrong in a way that shows up at tax time.

This post covers what Utah taxes apply, who collects them, and how to record them so your books tie out. I'm citing the Utah State Tax Commission's own publications throughout. Rates change, sometimes quarterly, so always confirm current rates at [tax.utah.gov/sales/rates](https://tax.utah.gov/sales/rates). This is a bookkeeping guide, not tax advice. Your CPA or the Tax Commission has the final word on your situation.

## Which Taxes Apply to Utah Short-Term Rentals?

According to the Tax Commission's [Publication 56, Sales Tax Information for Lodging Providers](https://tax.utah.gov/forms-pubs/pub-56/), amounts paid for temporary lodging are subject to **both sales tax and transient room tax (TRT)**.

"Temporary lodging" means accommodations in a hotel, motel, inn, tourist home, or similar property for **less than 30 consecutive days**. Stays of 30 consecutive days or longer are exempt from both sales tax and TRT.

### Sales tax

Sales tax on lodging is charged at the combined state and local rate where the rental is located, not where you live ([Publication 71](https://tax.utah.gov/forms-pubs/pub-71/)). Combined rates vary by city and county, so look up your property's location.

### Transient room tax

Pub 56 explains that a location's TRT rate can be a combination of up to six pieces:

1. A total **statewide TRT of 1.07%**.
2. An additional **0.25% in Salt Lake County**.
3. A **county-wide TRT of up to 4.5%** in every county except Salt Lake County.
4. A **4.75% county-wide total in Salt Lake County** (4.25% TRT plus a 0.5% tourism TRT).
5. A **city or town TRT of up to 1%**.
6. An **additional city or town TRT of up to 0.5%** where the city qualifies.

The statewide rate went up on July 1, 2025. [Tax Bulletin 10-25](https://files.tax.utah.gov/tax/bulletin/tb-10-25.pdf) explains that the 2025 Legislature (HB 456) added a 0.75% secondary statewide TRT on top of the existing 0.32%, plus another 0.25% in Salt Lake County. Some older articles and help pages still show only the 0.32% statewide rate, so don't rely on them.

### Which fees are taxable

Pub 56 includes a table of common lodging fees. A few that matter for short-term rentals:

| Fee | Sales tax | TRT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Room charges, under 30 consecutive days | Taxable | Taxable |
| Cleaning fees | Taxable | Taxable |
| Pet fees | Taxable | Taxable |
| Damage fees | Taxable | Taxable |
| Hot tub fees | Taxable | Taxable |
| Resort fees | Taxable | Taxable |
| Parking fees | Not taxable | Not taxable |
| Late payment fees | Not taxable | Not taxable |
| Lockout or lost key fees | Not taxable | Not taxable |

The takeaway: your cleaning fee is part of the taxable amount, not a separate untaxed charge.

## What Airbnb and VRBO Collect for You

Utah treats booking platforms as **marketplace facilitators**. Per [Publication 71](https://tax.utah.gov/forms-pubs/pub-71/):

- Facilitators that meet Utah's threshold (more than $100,000 of Utah sales in the previous or current calendar year) must collect sales tax **and all sales-related taxes, including transient room tax**, on the bookings they facilitate.
- **Sellers can't opt out** of having the facilitator collect.
- Sellers aren't liable for tax the facilitator was required to collect.
- A marketplace seller doesn't need a Utah sales tax license for facilitated sales, **unless** they have Utah nexus and also make sales outside a marketplace.
- A seller who does have a license must still file returns but doesn't report the sales made through a marketplace.

### Direct bookings change things

If you take bookings outside the platforms, through your own website, a repeat guest paying by Venmo, or a property management system's booking engine, no facilitator is collecting tax on those stays. Under Pub 71's language, that's when you need your own Utah sales tax license. Pub 56 says to report sales tax on form TC-62M or TC-62S and transient room tax on form **TC-62T**.

Check with your CPA or the Tax Commission about your filing frequency and whether any local business license or city rules apply. Many cities have their own short-term rental ordinances, separate from tax.

## How to Record Short-Term Rental Taxes in QuickBooks

Here's the principle: **taxes collected from guests aren't your income**, whether the platform remits them or you do. How you record them depends on who's remitting.

### Platform-remitted taxes

Your Airbnb payout is a net number. Behind each payout is gross booking revenue, cleaning fees, platform service fees, and any refunds or adjustments. When the platform collects and remits the tax, that money never reaches your bank account. Two common approaches:

- **Gross-up method (my preference):** record gross rental income and cleaning fee income, record the platform fee as an expense, and record the platform-remitted taxes in and out through a clearing account so they net to zero. Your books then show the true booking volume, and you can tie them to the platform's earnings reports.
- **Net method:** record income net of platform-remitted tax and leave the tax off the books entirely. It's simpler, but you lose the audit trail. Ask your CPA which they prefer for your return.

Either way, **don't record the bank deposit as one lump of "rental income."** A payout often covers several reservations and includes fee deductions. For the step-by-step on splitting payouts, see [short-term rental bookkeeping for Airbnb and VRBO hosts](/blog/short-term-rental-bookkeeping/).

### Taxes you collect yourself

For direct bookings, set up two liability accounts in QuickBooks Online, for example:

- **Sales Tax Payable – Utah**
- **Transient Room Tax Payable – Utah**

When a guest pays, record the room charge and cleaning fee as income and the taxes as liabilities. When you file and pay, clear the liability. If the liability account doesn't zero out after each filing period, something got missed. QuickBooks' sales tax center can handle the sales tax side for the property's location. Check whether it can handle your TRT rates too. If it can't, track TRT in its own liability account.

### Keep each property separate

Rates depend on the property's location, so a host with a condo in Salt Lake City and a cabin in another county is dealing with different TRT rates. Track each property as its own class or location in QuickBooks so you can pull revenue by property when it's time to file. My [rental property chart of accounts](/blog/chart-of-accounts-rental-properties/) post covers that setup.

## Month-End Checklist for Utah Short-Term Rental Hosts

- Download each platform's earnings or payout report for the month.
- Match every payout to the bank deposit.
- Confirm the platform shows tax collected and remitted on each Utah reservation.
- Record taxes on direct bookings to the liability accounts.
- Reconcile the liability accounts to what you filed and paid.
- Review profit and loss by property.

## FAQ

**Does Airbnb collect Utah transient room tax for me?**
Under Utah's marketplace rules in Pub 71, facilitators that meet the threshold must collect sales tax and sales-related taxes, including TRT, on bookings they facilitate. Check your platform's tax reports to confirm what was collected on each reservation.

**Is my cleaning fee taxable in Utah?**
Yes. Pub 56 lists cleaning fees as subject to both sales tax and transient room tax.

**What about stays of 30 days or longer?**
Pub 56 says lodging stays of 30 consecutive days or longer are exempt from both sales tax and TRT.

**Do I need a Utah sales tax license if I only use Airbnb?**
Pub 71 says marketplace sellers don't need a license for facilitated sales unless they have Utah nexus and also sell outside a marketplace. If you take any direct bookings, talk to your CPA about getting licensed.

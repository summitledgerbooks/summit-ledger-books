If you manage rentals for other owners in Utah, the rent, deposits, and reserves moving through your accounts aren't yours, and Utah's rules spell out how that money has to be held, recorded, and reconciled.

This post walks through the trust account sections of Utah Administrative Code Rule R162-2f from a bookkeeper's point of view, with the subsection for each requirement so you can check the source. It's a summary, not legal advice. For your specific situation, talk to your principal broker, a Utah real estate attorney, or the Division of Real Estate.

> **Heads up: Utah property management rules are changing.** A new property manager license takes effect January 1, 2027, and the Division has opened rulemaking on an amendment to R162-2f (comments accepted until December 1, 2026). Everything below reflects the rule text as published before that amendment. Check the [Division of Real Estate's property management page](https://commerce.utah.gov/realestate/property-management/) for the latest.

## Who Needs a Property Management Trust Account?

Under **R162-2f-403b(1)(a)**, the principal broker of a property management company must:

- **Manage 7 or more units:** set up at least one *property management trust account*. It has to be separate from the broker's real estate trust account and held at a bank or credit union located in Utah.
- **Manage 6 or fewer units, or also list and sell:** keep at least one *real estate trust account* at a Utah bank or credit union, under the general trust account provisions in R162-2f-403a.

When you open the account, move it to a different bank, or change the account number, **R162-2f-403b(1)(b)** requires you to notify the Division in writing within **ten business days**. The notice has to include the account number, the bank's address, and what the account is used for.

The Division's [account documentation guidance](https://commerce.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Real-Estate-account-requirements.pdf) also asks that the account name include the company name and "Property Management Trust Account," unabbreviated.

## What Money Goes In (and What Doesn't)

**R162-2f-403b(12)** says a property management trust account is used to secure:

- tenant security deposits,
- rents,
- money an owner gives you as a reserve fund or to pay unexpected expenses, and
- client funds from a regulated real estate transaction, if the broker hasn't set up a separate real estate trust account.

Two important exclusions:

- **Short-term rental money stays out.** Under **R162-2f-403b(3)**, funds from renting tourist accommodations for less than 30 consecutive days can't go into the property management trust account. If you manage both long-term and nightly rentals, the books need separate accounts for each.
- **Your own money is capped.** Under **R162-2f-403b(13)**, a broker who puts the company's own funds in the trust account has to track exactly how much belongs to the broker and do a monthly line-item reconciliation of those funds. Depositing more than **$10,000** of the broker's own money is a violation.

### Interest-bearing accounts

The account must be **non-interest-bearing** unless every condition in **R162-2f-403b(2)** is met, including written agreement and paying the interest to a 501(c)(3) that funds Utah affordable housing grants. For most small property managers, that means a plain non-interest-bearing account.

## Recording Deposits and Disbursements

This is where bookkeeping does the heavy lifting.

### Deposits

**R162-2f-403b(4)** requires every deposit record to show:

- a transaction number or unique client identifier,
- who paid and who it's for,
- the amount,
- the property's location, and
- the date and place of deposit.

In practice, every deposit gets tagged to an owner and a property, not recorded as one lump of rent.

### Disbursements

**R162-2f-403b(5)** lists what a check or other disbursement instrument must show, including the trust account name and number, the client identifier, payee, amount, and purpose. **Subsection (6)** makes allowances for instruments that can't carry every item, but the reconciliation rules still apply. Voided checks must be marked "void" and kept (**subsection 7**).

### When you're allowed to pay money out

Under **R162-2f-403b(14)**, you can only disburse trust funds as authorized by the management contract or lease, by other written authorization from the parties, by the rule itself, or by court order. **Subsection (15)** adds that for any transfer, your records have to show:

- you confirmed the money belonged to the owner you were spending it for,
- any fee or commission was actually earned under the management contract, and
- repair and maintenance transfers were authorized and used only for that purpose, with any excess returned to the trust account.

## Management Fees: The 60-Day Rule and the Operating Account

Two rules trip up small property managers more than any others:

1. **Move earned fees out within 60 days.** Leaving earned management fees in the trust account for more than 60 days after they're earned and received is a violation (**R162-2f-403b(13)(b)(ii)**).
2. **Fees go through the operating account first.** **R162-2f-403b(16)** says you can't pay a commission or your management fee straight from the trust account to someone else. The fee moves to your operating account first, and you pay from there.

For bookkeeping, that means a recurring monthly transfer of earned fees from trust to operating, recorded against each owner's ledger.

## Monthly Reconciliation Requirements

**R162-2f-403b(10)** is the core of trust accounting in Utah. For every trust account, the principal broker must:

- keep a **date-sequential record** of every deposit and disbursement, cross-referenced to the transaction or client identifier,
- keep a **current running balance**,
- keep records showing where each transaction's money ended up, and make sure **each closed transaction balances to zero**,
- reconcile the trust records to the **bank statement at least monthly**,
- reconcile the trust records to the **client accounts at least monthly**, and
- make the records available to the Division on request.

The rule doesn't use the term, but reconciling bank, book, and client ledgers monthly is what the industry calls a **three-way reconciliation**. If the three don't agree, you want to find out in the same month. I cover how the rent roll fits into this in [rent roll reconciliation](/blog/rent-roll-reconciliation/).

### If the account is out of balance

Under **R162-2f-403b(11)**, if your bank documents that the account is out of balance and you can't cure it within 30 days, you must notify the Division within that window.

### Unclaimed funds

If you can't disburse trust funds within three years after they were due, **R162-2f-403b(9)** requires you to send them to the State Treasurer's Office as unclaimed property.

## How Long to Keep Records

**R162-2f-401k** requires principal brokers to keep trust account records, including the monthly reconciliations. You can keep them physically at the principal business location (or a designated branch) or electronically in a system that complies with Utah's Uniform Electronic Transactions Act. They must be kept for at least **three calendar years** after the year the transaction closes or fails, or, for a lease, the year the lease ends. The Division can ask to inspect them at any time.

## What Changes in 2027

The Division's [property management FAQ](https://commerce.utah.gov/realestate/property-management/) explains that starting January 1, 2027, a new property manager license will cover residential rental management under Utah Code § 61-2f-411. Highlights relevant to bookkeeping:

- Licensed property managers generally must associate with at least one real estate trust account at a Utah bank or credit union (§ 61-2f-411(3)).
- There's a narrow exception if both the property management agreement and the lease say client funds don't have to be held in trust (§ 61-2f-411(4)). One without the other isn't enough.
- A property manager affiliated with a principal broker keeps client funds in the broker's trust account (§ 61-2f-411(5)).

The final rule is still being written, so don't build processes around details that aren't final.

## FAQ

**Can a bookkeeper handle trust accounting without a real estate license?**
The Division's FAQ lists people who exclusively perform bookkeeping and accounting among those exempt from the property management license requirement (§ 61-2f-202.5). The principal broker is still responsible for the trust account under R162-2f.

**Can I hold short-term rental funds in my property management trust account?**
No. R162-2f-403b(3) bars funds from tourist accommodation rentals under 30 consecutive days from the property management trust account.

**How often do I have to reconcile?**
At least monthly, against both the bank records and the client accounts (R162-2f-403b(10)(d) and (e)).

**Can I pay a maintenance vendor straight from trust?**
Yes, if the contract or owner authorizes it (R162-2f-403b(15)(c)). Your own management fee can't come straight from trust (subsection 16).

## What Catch-Up Bookkeeping Actually Involves

Catch-up bookkeeping means rebuilding your records for a period that was never properly recorded. For a landlord, that usually means:

- pulling every bank, credit card, and loan statement for the gap period,
- categorizing each transaction by property, entity, and expense type,
- reconciling every account month by month so balances tie to the statements,
- splitting mortgage payments into interest, principal, and escrow,
- moving security deposits out of income and into a liability account, and
- producing a profit and loss statement and balance sheet for each period.

If the chart of accounts is a mess or several LLCs share one bank account, cleanup comes first. The full scope is on my [catch-up bookkeeping](/services/catch-up-bookkeeping/) page.

## What Drives the Cost

### 1. How far back you need to go

More months means more statements, more transactions, and more reconciliations. It's the biggest single factor, which is why my pricing tiers are based on it.

### 2. Transaction volume

Ten transactions a month and two hundred a month are very different projects even over the same period. Short-term rentals usually have more volume than long-term rentals because of frequent payouts, cleaning fees, and supply runs.

### 3. Number of properties and entities

Each property needs its transactions tagged correctly. Each LLC needs its own set of books. If several entities share one bank account, untangling them takes time. See [bookkeeping across multiple LLCs](/blog/bookkeeping-multiple-llcs/) for why that matters.

### 4. How complete your records are

If you can download every statement and the transactions are mostly from business accounts, the work moves fast. Missing statements, personal and business spending on the same card, or cash payments with no receipts all add time.

### 5. Software and integrations

If you use property management software like AppFolio, Buildium, or Stessa, it has to agree with the bank and with QuickBooks. When it doesn't, the sync issues have to be fixed, not worked around. I wrote about why in [AppFolio and QuickBooks don't match](/blog/appfolio-quickbooks-integration/).

### 6. The deadline and the goal

A catch-up for a refinance, a tax deadline, or a sale may need to be done faster or to a higher standard of documentation. Tell me the goal upfront so the scope matches it.

## How Long It Takes

A few months of backlog typically takes a week or two. A year or more can take several weeks, depending on transaction volume and the number of entities. You'll get a realistic timeline before the project starts.

## How to Lower the Cost Before You Start

Most of these cost nothing but an afternoon:

1. **Gather your statements.** Download every bank, credit card, and loan statement for the gap period as PDFs. Missing statements are the number one cause of delays.
2. **Collect your mortgage and escrow paperwork.** Annual Form 1098s and escrow statements make splitting mortgage payments much faster.
3. **List your properties and entities.** Include addresses, which LLC owns each, and when each was bought or sold.
4. **Pull your leases and deposit records.** You'll need them to set up security deposit liabilities correctly ([here's why](/blog/security-deposits-bookkeeping/)).
5. **Flag anything personal.** If personal spending went through a business account, a quick list saves back-and-forth.
6. **Stop the bleeding.** Open separate business accounts now if you don't have them. Every month of mixed transactions you avoid is a month that doesn't need untangling later.

## Catch-Up vs. Doing It Yourself

Some landlords can catch up on their own, especially with one or two properties and good records. If you go that route, start with a solid [rental property chart of accounts](/blog/chart-of-accounts-rental-properties/), reconcile every month in order, and don't skip the balance sheet accounts like loans and deposits.

Hiring it out usually makes sense when:

- you're more than a few months behind,
- you have multiple properties or LLCs,
- you need the books for a refinance, sale, or tax deadline, or
- you've tried before and it stalled.

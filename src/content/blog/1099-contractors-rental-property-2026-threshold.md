If you paid a plumber, a roofer, or a handyman on your rentals or a flip this year, the 1099 question is coming in January. The rule most investors learned is "$600 or more, send a 1099." For payments made in 2026, that number changed.

This post covers what the new threshold is, which payments still need a form, which ones don't, and how to set up QuickBooks so the January report is a click instead of a scavenger hunt. I'm quoting the IRS's own pages throughout. This is bookkeeping education, not tax advice. Your CPA has the final word on your situation.

## The 2026 Change: $600 Became $2,000

The IRS page [Am I required to file a Form 1099 or other information return?](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/am-i-required-to-file-a-form-1099-or-other-information-return) now says it plainly for Form 1099-NEC:

- For payments made **before 2026**, the reporting threshold is **$600**.
- For payments made **in 2026**, the reporting threshold is **$2,000**.
- For payments made **after 2026**, the IRS points you to Pub. 1099 for the inflation-adjusted amount.

The [Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec) explain that the increase comes from P.L. 119-21 and applies to payments made after 2025, with inflation adjustments possible starting in 2027.

So the 1099s you filed in January 2026 (for 2025 payments) used $600. The ones you file in January 2027 (for 2026 payments) use $2,000.

Two things didn't change:

- **Backup withholding still triggers a form at any amount.** The IRS says you must file a 1099-NEC for anyone you withheld federal income tax from under the backup withholding rules, regardless of the amount.
- **Attorney gross proceeds on Form 1099-MISC (box 10) stay at $600.** That's settlement-type money paid to an attorney, not the attorney's fee for services, which goes on the 1099-NEC.

## Who Gets a 1099-NEC

Per the 1099-NEC instructions, you file one for each person you paid at least $2,000 in the course of your trade or business for:

1. Services performed by someone who isn't your employee, **including parts and materials**, or
2. Payments to an attorney.

That "including parts and materials" part matters for real estate. If your electrician's invoice is $1,400 of labor and $900 of wire and fixtures, that's $2,300 in the box, not $1,400.

For a landlord or flipper, the usual suspects:

- General contractors and subs on a rehab
- Handymen, cleaners, and turnover crews
- Landscapers and snow removal
- An unincorporated property manager's fees (more on rent below)
- Your attorney, for legal services

## What You Don't Have to 1099

The same instructions list payments that don't need a 1099-MISC or 1099-NEC. The ones that come up most for investors:

### Payments to corporations

The IRS says that, generally, payments to a corporation, **including an LLC taxed as a C or S corporation**, aren't reportable. Attorneys are the big exception: legal fees still get reported even if the firm is a corporation. A plain single-member LLC is usually treated as a disregarded entity, not a corporation, which is exactly why you need the vendor's W-9 to know which one you're dealing with.

### Card and app payments

From the instructions: payments made with a credit card, payment card, and certain third-party network transactions are reported on **Form 1099-K** by the payment settlement entity and "are not subject to reporting on Form 1099-MISC or Form 1099-NEC." If you paid your painter with a business credit card, the card processor's reporting covers it, not you. Cash, check, ACH, and bank-to-bank transfers are still yours to track.

### Merchandise and personal payments

Payments for merchandise, freight, storage, and similar items aren't reportable. Home Depot doesn't get a 1099. And the IRS says to report only payments made in the course of your trade or business. Personal payments aren't reportable. The guy who fixed the fence at your own house isn't part of this.

### Rent paid to a property manager

If tenants pay rent to your property manager, the manager doesn't get a 1099 from the tenant. The instructions say the real estate agent or property manager uses **Form 1099-MISC** to report the rent paid over to the owner. That's the form you may receive, not one you send.

## A Note on Landlords and "Trade or Business"

The whole 1099 requirement hangs on "in the course of your trade or business." Whether a given rental setup counts as a trade or business for 1099 purposes depends on your facts, and it's a question for your CPA, not your bookkeeper. My practical advice is the same either way: collect W-9s and track contractor payments from day one. If your CPA says you need to file, you're ready. If they say you don't, you lost ten minutes.

## Why the Higher Threshold Doesn't Mean Less Work

It's tempting to read $2,000 as "fewer forms, less tracking." In practice the tracking is the same. You still have to know every vendor's total for the year before you can say they're under $2,000. A handyman who did three $700 jobs across two properties is at $2,100. If his payments are spread across a few bank accounts and LLCs, nobody notices until it's late.

Two more reasons not to coast:

- **Each paying entity counts separately.** If you hold rentals in more than one LLC, each one that pays a vendor looks at its own totals. That's another reason to keep separate books per entity. I wrote about that setup in [bookkeeping for multiple LLCs](/blog/bookkeeping-multiple-llcs/).
- **E-filing kicks in at 10 forms total.** The [General Instructions for Certain Information Returns](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099gi) say that if you're required to file 10 or more information returns during the year, you must e-file, and the 10 counts across all form types combined. The IRS's free [IRIS portal](https://www.irs.gov/iris) handles that.

## The Deadline

Form 1099-NEC is due to the IRS **on or before January 31**, on paper or electronically, per the 1099-NEC instructions. If the date falls on a weekend or legal holiday, the due date is the next business day. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so forms for 2026 payments are due Monday, February 1, 2027. Form 1099-MISC (rents, other income) is due February 28 on paper or March 31 if you e-file.

January 31 comes fast after the holidays. If your books aren't current by mid-January, the 1099s become a rush job.

## How to Track Contractors in QuickBooks Online

Here's the setup I use, so January is a report instead of a project.

### 1. Mark 1099 vendors when you add them

In the vendor profile, check the box to track payments for 1099 and enter the business ID or SSN from their W-9. Do it when you add the vendor, not in January. If you don't have a W-9 yet, ask before the first check goes out. You have the most leverage before you've paid.

### 2. Map your expense accounts to the right box

QuickBooks' 1099 workflow asks which expense accounts feed box 1a (nonemployee compensation) on the 1099-NEC. For rentals and flips, that's usually contractor labor, repairs and maintenance, cleaning, landscaping, legal fees, and your rehab cost accounts. If your [chart of accounts follows Schedule E categories](/blog/schedule-e-expense-categories-quickbooks/), this mapping takes a few minutes.

### 3. Pay contractors through QuickBooks, not around it

A check written by hand and never entered, or a Venmo payment that lands in an uncategorized bucket, won't show up on the 1099 report. Every payment should hit the vendor, even if the bank feed brings it in.

### 4. Run the 1099 summary in December, not January

Pull a vendor expense report for the year, filter to 1099 vendors, and sort by total. Anyone between about $1,500 and $2,000 is worth a second look for missing December bills. Anyone over $2,000 without a W-9 is your to-do list. Card payments should drop out of the 1099 totals. Spot-check that they did.

This is the part of the job I like most as a software engineer. The work is a filter and a sort, and it only works if the data underneath is clean. If every payment is tied to a vendor and an account, the report does the thinking. If it isn't, no tool fixes it.

## Flippers: Watch the Rehab Bills

On a flip, a big share of the rehab money goes to trades, and much of it moves fast: a deposit here, a progress payment there, sometimes cash for a small job. Tag every payment to the property's class or project and the vendor at the time you pay it. You'll need that for job costing anyway, and it makes the 1099 totals accurate by default.

## FAQ

**What's the 1099-NEC threshold for 2026?**
$2,000 for payments made in 2026, per the IRS. It was $600 for payments made before 2026. For payments after 2026, the IRS says to check Pub. 1099 for the inflation-adjusted amount.

**Do I 1099 a contractor I paid by credit card?**
Generally no. The IRS instructions say card payments and certain third-party network transactions are reported on Form 1099-K by the payment settlement entity, not on a 1099-NEC.

**Do I 1099 an LLC?**
It depends on how the LLC is taxed. Payments to an LLC treated as a C or S corporation generally aren't reportable, except for attorneys. A single-member LLC is often a disregarded entity, so you'd report it. The W-9 tells you which.

**Do materials count toward the $2,000?**
Yes. The 1099-NEC instructions include parts and materials in payments for services.

**When are 1099-NECs due for 2026 payments?**
The deadline is January 31, but January 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so the IRS filing deadline moves to Monday, February 1, 2027.
